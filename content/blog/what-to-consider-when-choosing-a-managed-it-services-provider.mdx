The provider you pick decides who watches the systems, how fast someone answers, and whether the setup can grow with the company.

## Proactive support and monitoring

Ask who is watching the network, the servers, and the endpoints while they are still up. Continuous oversight is what shortens downtime and surfaces vulnerabilities early.

## Cybersecurity

The provider should run firewalls, endpoint protection, threat detection, and regular assessments, and should train the people who use the systems. Human error is still a leading cause of breaches.

## Scalability

Headcount and systems change as the company grows. The packages should be able to change with them over the years that follow.

## Clear service level agreements

Response times, uptime, and performance metrics should be written down. That written agreement is what you can hold the provider to.

## Local presence

Remote support covers a lot of tickets. A local team can also walk into the office, and they already know the environment.

## Planning past maintenance

Maintenance keeps today's systems running. Ask whether the provider will also plan the next investments, the process changes, and how the setup scales.

## Backup and recovery

Business continuity depends on automated backups, secure off-site storage, and a recovery process that has been tested.

## Transparent pricing

Ask how the bill is built. A flat rate covering the agreed work keeps the IT cost manageable. Complex or unpredictable billing does not.
